Total Graphite plc (TGR) Announces FY2026 Annual Results, Confirms Vatomina Production Halt for Optimisation and Restart Plans

Total Graphite plc (TGR) has released its audited financial results for the fiscal year ending 31 March 2026. The specialist natural graphite producer confirmed the reinstatement of its London Stock Exchange listing in March 2026, successfully recapitalised its balance sheet, and resumed operations in Madagascar. Annual production reached 2,964 Mt, up from 2,169 Mt in 2025, although operations at the Vatomina project are temporarily suspended to implement an optimisation programme.

Key Highlights

  • Total Graphite plc (TGR) published its Annual Report for the year ended 31 March 2026.
  • Following recapitalisation, the Company’s shares resumed trading on the London Stock Exchange in March 2026.
  • Group production totaled 2,964 Mt in 2026, compared to 2,169 Mt in 2025; Vatomina production is temporarily paused for optimisation initiatives.
  • Investors should monitor the progress of the Vatomina optimisation programme and the post-year-end Portfolio Optimisation Review.

Restoration of Listing and Stabilisation Phase Completed in March 2026

Total Graphite, a specialist natural graphite producer with operations in Madagascar and projects in Mozambique, was established in 2017 and listed on the London Stock Exchange in December 2020. Chairman C G St. John-Dennis described the year ending 31 March 2026 as a "defining period" during which the Company reinstated its LSE listing, strengthened its balance sheet through significant recapitalisation, and converted major liabilities into equity. The Board inherited a company facing operational, financial, and governance challenges and focused on stabilising the business and restoring investor confidence. Management considers this the completion of the stabilisation phase of its turnaround, now shifting focus from rebuilding the Company to expanding the business.

Vatomina Project Production and Temporary Suspension for Optimisation

The Group’s production for the year was 2,964 Mt, up from 2,169 Mt in 2025 but below the 7,096 Mt recorded in 2024. All production originated from the Vatomina project, while the Sahamamy site remained on care and maintenance since April 2024. Operations at Vatomina were paused in September 2025 to install new equipment and refurbish the plant, resuming at the end of March 2026 after a successful fundraising. Production has been temporarily halted again as an independent technical review finalises workstreams involving drilling, mine planning, ore handling, plant configuration, and processing efficiency. The Board describes this optimisation phase as essential to achieving higher production volumes and enhanced project economics over the long term.

Mozambique Concessions and Downstream Development Plans

The Group holds two flake graphite concessions in Mozambique. The Montepuez development project completed a Definitive Feasibility Study in 2017 indicating an NPV of US$146 million with permits for a 100,000tpa operation. Balama Central has a pre-feasibility study showing an NPV of US$177 million for a 58,000tpa operation, which requires updating. Both projects were inactive during the year due to insurgency-related force majeure, though a site visit occurred in May 2026. Additionally, the Group holds a 2017 US Anode pre-feasibility study envisioning a facility in Reno, Nevada, with an NPV of US$377 million. Total Graphite plans to update feasibility studies to reflect current market conditions and an optimised Montepuez mine plan.

Critical Mineral Demand and Execution Risks Highlighted

The company emphasises natural flake graphite as a critical mineral vital to the energy transition, battery production, and electrification. The announcement notes China’s dominance in advanced graphite production and its export restrictions, which have accelerated adoption of African flake graphite. Post-year-end, the Group initiated a Portfolio Optimisation Review to evaluate capital allocation across its assets. The Board recognises that realising its vision will require time and that investors will assess the Company based on delivery. Key execution risks include the temporary production suspension at Vatomina and the need for additional financing or a joint venture partner to reactivate Sahamamy. The announcement did not disclose detailed financial performance figures.
