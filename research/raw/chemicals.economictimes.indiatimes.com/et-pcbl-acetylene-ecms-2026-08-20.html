PCBL Chemical wins scheme clearance for ₹329 crore acetylene black plan

PCBL Chemical has received ECMS approval for an acetylene black project involving an investment of about ₹329 crore. The move could help build domestic capacity in a critical conductive material used in batteries and industrial applications, while supporting India’s supply-chain localisation push.
  • ETChemicals
  • Published On Aug 19, 2026 at 12:35 PM IST
PCBL Chemical has secured a key policy approval that could sharpen its position in India’s fast-growing advanced materials market, with the company’s acetylene black proposal now cleared under the Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme, or ECMS.
The approval, reported by Business Standard, covers a planned investment of about ₹329 crore and centres on a material that sits at the intersection of batteries, electronics and industrial manufacturing. Acetylene black is used as a conductive additive in lithium-ion batteries and in several other applications where electrical conductivity and material performance matter.
Why acetylene black matters for India’s supply chain
The significance of the project goes beyond a single manufacturing line. Conductive materials such as acetylene black are increasingly important as India pushes to localise more of the value chain around electric mobility, energy storage and advanced industrial inputs. By backing domestic capacity in this segment, the approval aligns with a broader policy push to reduce dependence on imports in strategic components and materials.
For manufacturers, the appeal is straightforward. A local source of a critical conductive material can help reduce lead times, improve supply resilience and lower exposure to overseas price swings. For policymakers, projects in this category support the longer-term goal of building an ecosystem that can serve both domestic demand and export opportunities.
What the ECMS clearance signals
The Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme is designed to encourage investment in India’s electronics supply chain, and approvals under the programme are often watched closely by investors and industry participants for clues on where the government sees industrial momentum building. In this case, the clearance gives PCBL Chemical a policy-backed route into a niche but strategically relevant segment of the advanced materials market.
According to Business Standard, the proposed project is also expected to generate skilled employment and deepen the company’s participation in global value chains. That matters because materials businesses increasingly compete not just on production scale, but on the ability to meet technical standards, reliability requirements and customer specifications across markets.
A move with implications beyond batteries
Although lithium-ion batteries are the most visible end-use, acetylene black has wider industrial relevance. It is used where conductivity, dispersion and performance consistency are critical, making it useful in several advanced manufacturing settings. That broader application base gives the project some insulation from the cyclicality that can affect single-end-use businesses.
The approval also reflects how India’s industrial policy is gradually moving from broad manufacturing promotion to more targeted support for specialised inputs. Instead of focusing only on final assembly or large headline projects, the ecosystem now increasingly depends on smaller but technically important materials that can anchor local value addition.
For PCBL Chemical, the opportunity lies in turning policy approval into a commercially viable business line. Execution will determine how quickly the project can move from announcement to output, how effectively it can serve battery and industrial customers, and how far it can help reduce import reliance in a segment where domestic depth remains limited.
As India’s battery and electronics manufacturing ambitions expand, projects such as this one are likely to draw more attention from supply-chain planners, investors and policymakers alike. The real test will be whether the new capacity can scale efficiently enough to become a dependable part of the country’s advanced materials base.
