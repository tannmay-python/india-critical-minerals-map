            Geology and Geomorphology of the exploration area                     
        6   Offshore Geomorphology West Sewell Ridge (WSR) is located in between the
                                   Forearc and Inner Volcanic arc in the southern part of
                                   Andaman Sea. WSR is a N-S trending ridge of rugged
                                   topography with inferred lineaments, faults and having
                                   steep and gentle flanks in the east and west respectively.
                                                                                  
                                   The ridge top is characterized by small fracture zone
                                   presently filled with ooze sediments. Gullies /erosional
                                   channels are more at the eastern flank of the mount rather
                                   than the western flank. WSR comprises mainly volcanic
                                   rocks (Basalt, Spilite, Dacite), ooze sediments, terrestrial
                                   influx, volcanic arc sediments and sporadic occurrence of
                                   polymetallic nodules and crusts. The area is cut across by
                                   major North-South trending faults and lineaments such as
                                   West Andaman Fault and Sumatra Fault System.   
            Seabed Morphology       Seabed is characterised by a N-S trending ridge of
                                    rugged topography with inferred lineaments, faults and
                                    having steep and gentle flanks in the east and west
                                    respectively. The ridge top is characterized by small
                                                                                  
                                    fracture zone presently filled with ooze sediments. Most
                                    of the area has having slope less than 5°, whereas a
                                    higher slope of more than 20° occurs in the eastern and
                                    western flanks.

            ROV videography details The ROV was deployed at the western flank, central part
                                    and eastern part of WSR between water depths 500 to
                                    1800 m. Polymetallic nodules and crusts were observed
                                    over the sediment-covered areas at water depths ranging
                                    from 556 to 1505 m. Massive rock exposures having Fe-
                                    Mn crusts were observed generally on the western flank
                                    and the eastern moderate terraces of WSR. In between
                                    areas are filled with sediment, foraminiferal ooze and
                                    massive rock boulders were also observed.
