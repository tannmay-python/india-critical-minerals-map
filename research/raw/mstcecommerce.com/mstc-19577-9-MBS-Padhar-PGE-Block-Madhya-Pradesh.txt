        4   Exploration Details                                                   
        i.  Regional Geology             The Betul belt extends for a length of about 135
                                     km with an average width of 15km from Chhindwara
                                     town in the east to Chicholi village in Betul district in
                                     the west and is composed of volcano-sedimentary
                                     rocks, intruded by mafic-ultramafic and granitic suite of
                                     rocks in that order. The belt, which is characterized by
                                     bimodal volcanic rocks, is distinguished from the
                                     unimodal volcanics (mafic volcanics) of Mahakoshal
                                     belt and volcanicfree Sausar belt. The Betul belt forms
                                     an inlier surrounded by younger Gondwana sediments
                                     in the north and west and the Deccan Trap in the south
                                     and east.                                    
                                                                                  
                                             The litho-assemblage of the Betul belt
                                     comprises three distinct suites of rocks namely, 1)
                                     supracrustal rocks, which include quartzites, metapelite,
                                     bimodal volcanics (basalt-rhyolite) metaexhalites, calc-
                                     silicates and BIF which show evidence for shallow
                                     water sedimentation 2) ultramafic- mafic suite,
                                     represented by pyroxenite – hornblende, pyroxenite –
                                     gabbro – diorite - quartz diorite association and 3) syn
                                     to post tectonic granitic suites.            
                                         The northern part of Betul belt is dominated by
                                     Mafic -Ultramafic complex, known as Padhar   
                                     ultramafic complex; comprises a central ultramafic part,
                                     surrounded on all sides by mafic rocks, such as
                                     gabbros, hornblende gabbros, anorthositic gabbros, and
                                     diorites. The central ultramafic zone consists of
                                     harzburgites, olivine- websterite, websterite,
                                     pyroxenites, norites, and hornblendites. The rocks
                                                                                  
                                     display a great variability in grain-size, texture, and
                                     mineralogical composition and show gradation to
                                     gabbros, hornblende gabbros, anorthositic gabbros and
                                     diorites away from the main ultramafic part.Younger

                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                     homophanous granite intrusives and enclaves of older
                                     basement gneiss (Amla Gneiss) and supracrustals are
                                     also exposed at places.                      
                                         The  metasedimentary / supracrustal litho
                                     association dominate the western and north western
                                     part of the belt around Sonaghati and Chicholi areas
                                     whereas the volcano sedimentary sequence dominates
                                     the eastern and central parts. The mafic-ultramafic
                                     complex crops out mainly in the western and north
                                     western part of the belt around Padhar and in the
                                                                                  
                                     eastern part of the belt around Mordongri where it
                                     occurs in association with bimodal volcanics. Apart
                                     from these mafic-ultramafic complexes, there are
                                     several other mapable units of gabbro, pyroxenite and
                                     hornblendite which also occur in association with
                                     bimodal volcanics. Granitoids show both intrusive and
                                     tectonic contact relationship with the supracrustals and
                                     mafic-ultramafics. Several ENE-WSW trending ductile
                                     shear zones have often served as avenues for 
                                     emplacement of granitic rocks.               
        ii. Geochemical Investigation Major part of the proposed area falls in parts of
                                     toposheet no 55F/16 and NGCM survey has been 
                                     carried out during FS 2008-2010. In toposheet
                                     no.55F/16, it is observed from the geochemical contour
                                     maps showing distribution of oxides/elements, that the
                                     oxides like MgO, MnO, CaO and elements like Cr, Nb,
                                                                                  
                                     Zr, V, Ni and Y show some definite pattern of their
                                     disposition which are mainly dependent on the
                                     underlying lithology. Whereas, some other oxides like
                                     Fe2O3, Na2O, and elements like Cu, Ba, show random
                                     distribution pattern and cover different rock types. The
                                     analytical results of gold in all the composite stream
                                     sediment samples are below its lower detection limit (3
                                     ppb). However, three samples (55F16/S/2, 3, &4/08-09)
                                     show values 3 ppb, 5 ppb and 3 ppb respectively. These
                                     three samples fall over ultrabasic rock of Proterozoic
                                     Betul Belt. As far as soil samples are concerned, it is
                                     observed that the values of SiO2, Na2O, TiO2, MnO &
                                     Sr in “R‟ samples are incremental compared to “C‟
                                     horizon samples, whereas for Al2O3, MgO, CaO,
                                     Fe2O3, V, Rb the response is opposite.       
                                                                                  
                                      The contour pattern of Nb, Pb and Au (Sample No
                                     55F12/135/S/09-10 with 15 ppb) in 55F/12 is  
                                     interesting as highest values for these elements are
                                     close to a regional curvilinear fault in the northern part
                                     of the area. The dispersion of these elements seems to
                                     be structurally controlled. The dispersion pattern of
                                     elements like MnO, Na2O, Sc, Co and Ba is also
                                     having an influence of the regional curvilinear fault
                                     where high value contours can be seen which are
                                     almost parallel to this fault. The chromium content in
                                     55F/16 varies from 42 to 769 ppm with an average of
                                     142.58 ppm. Values above 200 ppm are considered as
                                     contamination.         5   Quantity of Minerals (Grade-wise) Geomysore Services (India) Pvt. Ltd carried out
                                     extensive work in the Padhar mafic-ultramafic
                                     complex. Rock-chip sample east of Dhappa has 
                                     analyzed 0.5% Ni, 0.11% Cu, 70ppb Pb and 68 ppb Pd.
                                     The channel profiles show significant Ni- Cu-PGE
                                     anomalous values up to 0.38% Ni and 0.1 g/t Pt+Pd.
                                     Rock-chip samples collected around Bacha have
                                     yielded highly anomalous PGE values with maximum
                                                                                  
                                     value of 0.36 g/t and 0.37 g/t Pt+Pd.        
                                      Channels cut along 3 profiles in the Bacha-Baretha
                                     area have analyzed significant values for Ni-Cu-PGE.
                                     Profile-1 shows a first 4m section of the 12 m anomaly
                                     zone with samples analyzing 0.14-0.46g/t Pt+Pd
                                     (Av.0.28 g/t Pt+Pd). A 7m anomalous zone was 
                                     encountered where Pt ranged between 13-26 ppb, Pd
                                     ranged between 53-148 ppb.                   
                                      In RMT work carried out by GSI, one bed rock
                                     sample analyzed the Ni values up to 0.403% Ni and
                                     1760 ppm Cr from Dhappa area in pyroxenite rocks.
        i.  Mineral Commodity         PGE and Nickle                              
        ii  Exploration Potential of Block Proposed block is the part of Padhar mafic-ultramafic
                                     complex, occurs in the western and northwestern parts
                                     of the Betul Belt. This is a well differentiated complex
                                                                                  
                                     mainly exposing mafic-ultramafic rocks in the proposed
                                     area. Analytical results are showing highly anomalous
                                     PGE, Ni, Co and associated critical elements values in
                                     the ultramafic rocks. A few grains of pentlandite,
                                     pyrrhotite, chalcopyrite, gold and Ir-PGM are reported
                                     from altered Cr-spinel, talc-chlorite and anorthosite
                                     gabbro. In the Padhar mafic-ultramafic complex, the
                                     concentration of Ir-PGE may be explained by the
                                     accumulation of Ir-rich alloys that segregated directly
                                     from the melt at an early stage in the evolution of the
                                     complex.                                     
                                      Based on field evidences, petrography, mineral
                                     chemistry, trace element geochemistry and chrome-
                                     spinel chemistry, Padhar mafic-ultramafic complex is
                                     characterized as Alaskan type of mafic-ultramafic
                                     complex. In general, they are good prospective for PGE
                                     mineralization. Chemical analysis for PGE and
                                                                                  
                                     associated mineralization of the Padhar mafic-
                                     ultramafic complex is also showing potentiality. So
                                     further detailed study in altered mafic-ultramafic is
                                     warranted to access Ni-PGE potentiality in Padhar
                                     mafic-ultramafic complex. Based on the above 
                                     geological investigation an area about 729.18 sq km
                                     could be demarcated as anomalous area for further
                                     exploration for PGE and associated mineralization.

        8   Hydrogeological information                                           
         i  Local Surface Drainage Pattern The drainage in the area forms a part of regime of the
            (Channels)               Machana River system. The area is drained by several
                                     major (Nimpani) and minor streams and several
                                     ephemeral streamlets which are lower order tributaries
                                     forming sub-dendritic pattern and ultimately flowing into
                                     the Machana River.                           
        ii  Type of aquifers         Occurrence and movement of ground water in hard rocks
                                     is mainly controlled by secondary porosity through joints
                                                                                  
                                     and fractures.                               
        iii Rivers/Streams           Machana River and its tributaries.           
        iv  Sea Coast                Not present.                                 
        9   Climate                                                               
                                                                                  
         i  Annual Rainfall (Avg.)   Rainfall in the area varies from moderate to fairly high
                                     with maximum precipitation reaching up to 284.9mm,
                                     mostly accounted during July and August, which are the
                                     wettest months.                              
        ii  Temperature (December)   The study area experiences temperate climate with fairly
                                     cold weather having maximum temperature of around
                                     27°C and minimum temperature of around 14°C between
                                     November and January. Summer is considerably hot. It
                                     lasts from April to June with maximum temperature
                                     rising up to 41°C. May and June are the hottest months.
                                                                                  
        10  Topography               The study area has exhibits highly rugged    
                                     terrain/topography dotted with hillocks and ridges around
                                     west of Kevlajhir, north of Bacha and Temra villages.
                                     Isolated hillocks can be seen all around in Temra,
                                     Khokra, Khadara and Bacha areas. Quartz reefs,
                                     granitoids and mafic-ultramafic rocks exhibit highly
                                                                                  

                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                     elevated topography in the area. The elevation (RL)
                                     difference varies from 892m to 420m. The plain area is
                                     occupied by weathered mafic and ultramafic bodies and a
                                     patch of Gondwana sediments.
