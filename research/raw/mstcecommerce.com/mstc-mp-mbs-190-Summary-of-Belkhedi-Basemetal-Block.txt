                Mineral Zones Systematic geochemical soil sampling on 100 x 20 m interval was
                             carried out by Praveen and Senthil kumaran during the year 2006-07, in
                             Belkhedi Block and has resulted in delineating anomalous zones for Zn
                             and Pb. Two E – W-trending, 1000 ppm Zn anomaly zones were
                             established. The main Zn anomaly extends for 400m with an average
                             width of 50m. Another 1000 ppm Zn anomaly has been established with
                             a dimension of 200m x 50m. An E – W-trending 500 ppm Pb anomaly
                             is also marked with a dimension of 250m x 40m.         
                             Later during the year 2014-15, Biswajit Lenka in FS 2014 -15 had
                             established a 200 ppm Pb anomaly which encompasses the 500 ppm Zn
                             anomaly in the western most part of the area mapped. The detailed
                             mapping of 1.2 sq km was carried out with detail geophysical survey
                             revealed presence of pink silicified felsic rhyolite with alteration zones
                             represented by garnetiferous quartz-sericite-chlorite-biotite rock of
                             Banskhapa Formation. The host rock for basemetal mineralization is
                             identified as garnetiferous quartz-sericite-chlorite-biotite rock. The
                             present work has also established a 200 ppm Pb anomaly which is
                             encompassing the 500 ppm Zn anomaly in the western most part of the
                             area mapped. Some bedrock samples in anomaly area analysed high Zn
                             and Pb values.

              7 Hydrography                                                         
                Local surface The area mainly exhibit undulatory topography and forms a small part
                drainage pattern of the regime of the Kanhan river system and comprises undulating
                (Channels)   land surface sloping towards the south.                
                Rivers/Stream Lower order tributaries of the Kanhan River drain the area forming a
                             sub-dendritic pattern, prominent among them is the Batiyadev Nala
                             which flows south to meet the Kanhan River east of Bhuyari village.

                Morphology of Physiographically, the area forms a small part of the regime of the
                the area     Kanhan river system and comprises undulating land surface sloping
                             towards the south. Resistant, massive felsic volcanic rock form E-W
                             trending linear mounds and hillocks. The highest elevation (737 m) in
                             the area is a hillock located south of Belkheri village and lowest
                             elevation is in the Batiyadev Nala at 660 m.
