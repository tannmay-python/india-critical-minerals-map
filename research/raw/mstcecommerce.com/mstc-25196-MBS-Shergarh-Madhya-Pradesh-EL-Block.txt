         4   Exploration Details                                                  
         i.  Regional Geology         The Bundelkhand Granitoid Complex (BGC) dominates the
                                      region, consisting of diverse plutonic and hypabyssal rocks,
                                      primarily granites of multiple generations, gneisses,
                                      migmatites, and leucogranites. It includes enclaves of older
                                      metabasics, banded magnetite quartzite, amphibolite, and
                                                                                  
                                      meta-ultramafics, with sporadic occurrences of schists.
                                      Hypabyssal rocks, such as porphyries, quartz reefs, and
                                      dolerites, are also present. The massif is bordered by Middle
                                      Proterozoic Bijawar Group sediments and volcanics to the
                                      south and southeast, Upper Proterozoic Vindhyan
                                      Supergroup sediments to the west, and Middle Proterozoic
                                                                                  
                                      Gwalior Group rocks to the northwest. To the north, the
                                      BGC extends beneath the Indo-Gangetic alluvium, while the
                                      northwest is covered by Chambal alluvium. Additionally,
                                      parts of the BGC and sedimentary formations are overlain
                                      by Cretaceous to Lower Eocene Deccan Trap basalts.
         ii. Geochemical Investigation The NGCM program was executed during the 2015-2016
                                      field season. Elevated vanadium (V) values, reaching up to
                                                                                  
                                      142 ppm, have been recorded in the southwestern part of the
                                      proposed area. Additionally, sporadic distributions of
                                      hafnium (Hf) values, up to 52 ppm, and copper (Cu) values,
                                      up to 40 ppm, have been observed in the southern and north-
                                      northwestern (NNW) parts of the proposed block.

         ii  Exploration Potential of Block  The geological setup of the proposed block is similar to
                                         that of the Panna Diamond Belt. Furthermore, the
                                         Kaimur Formation, which is exposed in this area,
                                         features the development of laterite near the boundary
                                         between the BGC and the Vindhyan Supergroup.
                                       The prominent circular shaped prominent gravity high
                                         along with magnetic high in the north-eastern part of the
                                        EL block is indicating heavy rock mass in a confined
                                                                                  
                                         cylindrical structure which may be the locale of possible
                                         pipe like intrusion in Vindhyan sediments.
                                       Apart from that in the Exploration License (EL) block,
                                         several prominent circular gravity highs, coupled with
                                         magnetic highs, have been identified. These anomalies
                                         suggest the presence of heavy rock masses in confined
                                         cylindrical structures.                  
                                       Rio Tinto Exploration India Private Limited conducted
                                         extensive exploration work in the Bunder Prospecting
                                         License area within Chhatarpur district, Madhya
                                         Pradesh, from 2006 to 2011. Their investigations
                                         revealed numerous magnetic anomalies, leading to the
                                         discovery of several kimberlite pipes near the boundary
                                         between the Bundelkhand Granite Complex (BGC) and
                                                                                  
                                         the Vindhyan Supergroup. These findings further
                                         support the interpretation of prominent circular gravity
                                         highs and magnetic anomalies within the EL block,
                                         indicating the potential presence of heavy rock masses
                                         in  confined cylindrical structures, possibly
                                         representing pipe-like                   
                                       intrusions in the Vindhyan sediments.

         7   Accessibility                                                        
                                                                                  
         i.  Nearest Railway Station  Jhansi Junction Railway Station             
         ii. Road                     The area is well-connected by an extensive network of
                                      national highways. NH-3 (also known as the Agra-Mumbai
                                      Road or A-B Road) passes through the western part of
                                                                                  
                                      Toposheet No. 54G/14, connecting Gwalior to Shivpuri.
                                      Additionally, NH-25 links Gwalior to Jhansi, and NH-75
                                      connects Jhansi to Shivpuri. Gwalior, located 130 km to the
                                      north, and Shivpuri, 55 km to the southeast of Shergarh, are
                                      the major nearby cities providing access to the area. The
                                      region is further accessible through a network of metalled
                                      and unmetalled roads, cart tracks, and footpaths, which
                                                                                  
                                      originate from                              
                                      or are linked to NH-3 and the Shivpuri-Jhansi Road.

         10  Topography               Geologically the Eastern and South-eastern part of the
                                      area is underlain by Bundelkhand Granite Complex
                                      which forms a peneplained terrain with a few isolated
                                      hillocks and ridges mostly made up of quartz reefs. On the
                                      west, Bundelkhand Granite is overlain by the rocks of the
                                      Vindhyan Supergroup, which forms steep escarpments. At
                                      places, they form flat and slightly undulatory plateaus over
                                      the granites.
