         4   Exploration Details                                                  
                                                                                  
         i.  Regional Geology      Regionally, the study area forms part of the Trans-Aravalli
                                   region, exposed to the west and southwest of the Aravalli
                                   Mountain Range. The geology is dominated by the Malani
                                   Igneous Suite (MIS), the largest predominantly acidic,
                                   volcanic–plutonic magmatic assemblage in India, representing
                                   polyphase Neoproterozoic igneous activity. The MIS was
                                   emplaced during a time interval between the deposition of the
                                   Sirohi Group and the Marwar Supergroup.        
                                                                                  
                                   The Malani Igneous Suite unconformably overlies the
                                   Mesoproterozoic rocks of the Delhi Supergroup and is
                                   nonconformably overlain by sedimentary sequences of the
                                   Marwar Supergroup. The Siwana volcanic–plutonic complex,
                                   an integral part of the MIS, records three distinct magmatic
                                   phases: (i) an early peralkaline volcanic phase forming the basal
                                   lava flows, (ii) a middle metaluminous volcanic phase
                                                                                  
                                   comprising the upper flows, and (iii) a late peralkaline intrusive
                                   phase represented by the Siwana Granite.       
                                                                                  
                                   The Siwana Granite, genetically related to mantle-derived
                                   magmatism, is broadly contemporaneous with the Jalore
                                   Granite and intrudes the Siwana rhyolites. These intrusive and
                                   extrusive units collectively define the Siwana Ring Complex
                                   (SRC), characterized by arcuate to ring-shaped outcrop patterns.
                                   The volcanic flows display elliptical geometries with gentle
                                   inward dips toward the center of the ring structure, reflecting
                                   caldera-related emplacement and subsidence.    
         ii. Geochemical Investigation The proposed area falling within Toposheet No. 45C/10 was
                                   previously covered under the NGCM program during the field
                                   season 2018–19. Elevated concentrations of REE elements
                                   around southern part of Phulan village and north of Mokalsar
                                                                                  
                                   village were recorded which is primarily associated with the
                                   Siwana peralkaline granite.                    
                                                                                  

                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
         iii. Geophysical Investigation Toposheet No. 45C/10 was covered under National
            (NGPM)                 Geophysical Mapping Programme (NGPM) during FS 2021–
                                   22. The block exhibits distinct geophysical signatures indicative
                                   of REE potential. Bouguer gravity data reveal a prominent high-
                                   intensity anomaly extending from Mokalsar to Kathari and
                                   eastward towards Bharwani, with moderate gravity highs within
                                   the block, suggesting subsurface lithological heterogeneity.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                   Magnetic data record a high-amplitude anomaly ranging from
                                   100 nT to 480 nT in the central part of the block around Ratri,
                                   Majal, and Gelawas villages, indicating the presence of mafic–
                                   alkaline phases commonly associated with REE-enriched
                                   peralkaline granites and rhyolites of the Malani Igneous Suite.
                                   Another NW–SE-trending high magnetic anomaly is observed
                                   near Mokalsar, northwest of the block.         
                                                                                  
                                   The close spatial correlation between high-intensity gravity and
                                   magnetic anomalies suggests upwarping of dense mafic–
                                   ultramafic rocks from the lower crust, providing a favourable
                                   geodynamic setting for alkaline–peralkaline magmatism and
                                   associated REE mineralisation potential within the block.

         i.  Mineral Commodity     Rare Earth Elements                            
         ii  Exploration Potential of Block As on FS 2024–25, a total of 16 exploration items has been
                                   completed in the Siwana Ring Complex (SRC), comprising 7
                                   G-4, 6 G-3, 2 G-2 and 1 STM items. Additionally, 4 G-3 items
                                   are under execution during FS 2025–26, of which two are
                                   contiguous to the present EL block, indicating systematic
                                   upgradation of SRC from reconnaissance to advanced
                                   exploration stages.                            
                                                                                  
                                   Multiple investigations across the SRC have consistently
                                   reported anomalous to high ΣREE+Y values in peralkaline
                                   granites, rhyolites, felsite dykes and younger intrusive, with
                                   concentrations ranging from approximately 1000 ppm to greater
                                                                                  
                                   than 2 wt%, and locally up to about 3.5 wt% ΣREE+Y in
                                   channel samples. The mineralization is characteristically
                                   LREE-dominant and is commonly associated with Zr, Nb, Hf,
                                   Th, U, Rb, Zn and Ba, reflecting a typical alkaline–peralkaline
                                   rare-metal geochemical signature.              
                                                                                  
                                   G-4 and G-3 investigations in Bhimgoda Pahar, Koiliyasar-Ka-
                                   Pahar, Siwana East–Central, Kalaur Ka Danta, Chhapan Ka
                                   Pahar, Indrana–Siwana, Sainji ki Beri–Meli and Nimle ki
                                   Pahari– Dantala areas have established widespread surface and
                                   subsurface REE enrichment, with ΣREE+Y commonly
                                   exceeding 2000 ppm in bedrock samples and greater than 1 wt%
                                   in felsite dykes and younger intrusive.        
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  

                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
                                   Recent G-3 stage exploration in the North of Kalaur Ka Danta
                                   block has resulted in estimation of in-situ inferred resources of
                                   TREE/TREO, Nb, Zr and Rb over limited areas (<1 sq km),
                                   confirming grade continuity and subsurface persistence of REE
                                   and associated rare metals. Similarly, G-3 exploration in the
                                   WNW  of Sukleswar Ka Mandir area has established UNFC-
                                   compliant inferred resources of TREE, TREE+Y, Nb, Hf and Zr
                                   using longitudinal vertical projection and cross-section
                                                                                  
                                   methods.                                       
                                                                                  
                                   The present EL block lies adjacent to several positively
                                   explored and resource-bearing blocks within the SRC and
                                   occupies the same peralkaline volcanic–plutonic domain. The
                                   spatial proximity, geological continuity, consistent lithological
                                   association and robust geochemical signatures collectively
                                   indicate high potential for REE and associated rare-metal
                                   mineralization, justifying systematic G-3 stage exploration in
                                   the block.
