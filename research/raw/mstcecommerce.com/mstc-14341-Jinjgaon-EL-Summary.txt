         i. Regional Geology          The rocks exposed in Kothi, Vishamundi and Jinjgaon areas
                                      belong to Bengpal and Surjagarh Groups and are
                                                                                  
                                      characterized by diopside-quartzite, sericite-fuchsite-garnet
                                      quartzite/quartz schist, banded magnetite quartzite, quartz-
                                      kyanite rock, amphibolite, hornblende schist, talc-tremolite
                                      actinolite schist occurring as bands and enclaves in gneissic
                                      and migmatitic rocks. Homogenization in migmatite is so
                                      over-whelming that at times it is difficult to establish their
                                      inter-relationship in the area. These rocks are later intruded
                                      by dolerite, ultramafite and gabbro dykes. The Bengpal
                                      Group is overlain by green-stone suite of rocks i.e.
                                                                                  
                                      characterized by meta ultramafite-serpentinite association
                                      (Alpine type) with ferruginous chert, Banded haematite
                                      quartzite and basic meta volcanic. These rocks are later
                                      intruded by dolerite and quartz veins. The pyritiferous cherty
                                      quartzite which was previously included in Bengpal Group
                                      (Saxena et.al 1979) is now considered to form part of
                                      Surjagarh Group only as it is intimately associated with
                                      ultramafite and ferruginous cherty sequence. The Surjagarh
                                      Group in this area occurs in a long linear NE-SW trending
                                      belt which takes an E-W swing further south west and then
                                                                                  
                                      swerve to NW-SE forming a limb of antiformal structure
                                      parallel to main Surjagarh antiformal closer.
