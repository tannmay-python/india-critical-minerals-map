           7  Hydrography                                                         
                                  In Tirupati district there are 09 minor river basins i.e.,
              Local Surface Drainage                                              
                                  Swarnamukhi, Kalangi, Upputeru, Cheyyeru, Arani, Palar,
              Pattern (Channels)                                                  
                                  Kushustali, Minor Drainages between Arani & Kalangi and
                                  Minor Drainages between Swarnamukhi & Kalangi river
                                  basins. The Swarnamukhi, Kalangi and Upputeru river basins
                                  cover 80% of the district and the remaining part of the
                                  district is partially covered with Arani, Cheyyeru, Kushustali
                                  and Palar River basins. The Palar basin (Ponnai, Kaundinya
              Rivers/Streams                                                      
                                  Nadi and Malattar rivers) covers about 80% of the Chittoor
                                  district. The remaining 20% the area is covered by the
                                  Pennar basin (Bahuda and Pincha rivers), Swarnamukhi
                                  basin, Ponnair basin, Araniyar basin, Kushustali basin
                                  (Nagari river)                                  
                                  The temperature in the western parts of the district is
                                  relatively lower than the eastern parts of the Chittoor
                                  district. This is because of the higher altitude of the western
           8                      parts compared to the eastern parts. Most of the district has
                                  a tropical wet and dry climate, with some north-western
              Climate             parts having hot semi-arid climate. The south-west storm
                                  that takes after the midyear season keeps going up to the
                                  second seven day stretch of October. The withdrawing
                                  North-east rainstorm begins from mid-October to the finish
                                  of November. The period from December to mid-February
                                  appreciates for the most part a fine climate.   
                                  The average annual rainfall of the district is 913 mm, of
                                  which 453 mm falls as South-West (June-September)
                                  monsoon and 428 mm as North- East (October-December)
              Mean Annual Rainfall                                                
                                  monsoon. The district receives high amount of rainfall from
                                  North-East Monsoon or Retreating monsoon (November-
                                  December).                                      
                                  The summer temperature touches 44oC in the eastern part,
                                  whereas the western part ranges between 36oC to 38oC.
              Temperatures        Similarly, the winter temperatures in the western parts are
                                  relatively low ranging between 12oC and 14oC and in eastern
                                  parts it is 16oC to 18oC.
