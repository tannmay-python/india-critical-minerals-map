                                    Soil Samples: A total of 256 soil samples collected.
                                    Cu: 5 to 960ppm; Pb: 5 to 160ppm; Zn: 20ppm to 2100 ppm.
                                    Geochemical Survey: In order to understand the pattern and
                                    intensity of secondary dispersion of the metal ions in the area, the
                                    soil sampling was carried out systematically in a grid pattern
                                    covering the host rock. For this purpose, a base line was drawn
                                    along strike of the host rock and traverse line across it at a spacing
                                    of 200m. Sample points were located at 20m intervals along
                                    traverse lines. Soil samples were collected from B-horizon of the
                                    soil profile which is fairly well developed in the area between 20
                                    to 60 cm depth.                                 
                                    The analytical results of 256 soil samples collected from 06
                                    sampling lines i.e from 00 to E80 and 33 soil samples of Ryatwari
                                    temple area which was collected in F.S 2003-04 show dispersion
                                    of Cu, Pb and Zn in varying degree. The Copper values range
                                    from less than 5ppm to 960 ppm, Lead from less than 5ppm to
                                    160 ppm and Zinc from 20ppm to 2100ppm, Nickle from less
                                    than 5 ppm to 100 ppm with one single value of 295 ppm, Cobalt
                                    from less than 5 ppm to 60 ppm, Silver and Cadmium all less than
                                    5 ppm.                                          
                                    Zinc: The minimum value of zinc is found to be 20ppm while

                 Highlights of Ground Based on the integration of different Geophysical surveys of
                 Geophysical Survey electrical (resistivity and chargeability), magnetic & spontaneous
                                    polarisation the following conclusions were made:
                                    The strong E-W oriented magnetic contours situated north of the
                                    baseline between W40 and E150 indicate a part of regional
                                    geological structure passing through the present area. This zone
                                    in association with resistivity values less than 3000 ohm-mts and
                                    the chargeability values less than 12 msec may not carry any
                                    sulphide mineralisation but may contain iron rich minerals due to
                                    high magnetic contrast. The association of magnetic rich minerals
                                    can be located in this zone only compare to the other areas under
                                    survey. In general, the resistivity values less than 300 Ohm-mt
                                    occupy 70 - 75% of the area is corroborated with the low
                                    chargeability values less than 10 msec may reveal absence of
                                    abundance of sulphide mineralisation. However, the high
                                    chargeability values covering a small portion of the area ranging
                                    from 17 to 24 msec is interpreted as a geological feature orienting
                                    NE-SW situated north of the baseline between W40 and W160. It
                                    is also associated with resistivity values less than 3000 Ohm-mts.
                                    This feature may be holding sulphide mineralisation in the
                                    existing Rhyolitic terrain as the chargeability increases drastically
                                    within 200 X 300 mts. The chances of occurrence of zinc
                                    mineralisation at this zone of outer periphery of the chargeability
                                    contour may not be ruled out. It is also observed that the zone of
                                    high resistivity values more than 3000 Ohm-mts associated with
                                    high chargeability values ranging between 15 and 20 msec may
                                    also contain highly disseminated sulphide mineralisation like the
                                    zone with station interval S20 to S40 through E40 and E80 of
                                    traverses as well as the zone with station interval S10 to S20
                                    between E100 and E150 traverses. A NNW-SSE trending SP low
                                    between E20 and E70 indicate the formational change and may
                                    be due to variation of ground water table in the area.

                 Highlights of the Petrographical/ During the ore petrographic studies, sulphide mineralization is
                 Petrochemical Studies found in rocks mainly in garnetiferous- quartz-biotite schist and
                                    in foliated rhyolites. Mineralisation occurs in form of
                                    disseminated specks, blebs and rare veinlets comprising of pyrite,
                                    chalcopyrite and sphalerite.                    
                                    Pyrite occurs in two forms namely, 1) minute discrete grains with
                                    nearly euhedral outline. 2) This type of pyrite is invariably seen
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    

                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                    as amoebic particles in close association with chalcopyrite and
                                    sphalerite.                                     
                                    Chalcopyrite occurs as a minor phase in the sulphide veinlets.
                                    They have embayed margins both against pyrite and sphalerite.
                                    No exsolution features are observed in the Chalcopyrite.
                                    Sphalerite occurs in the sulphide veinlets and form the major
                                    component. The sphalerite has pyrite and chalcopyrite as
                                    inclusions and also appear to have replaced them towards the
                                    margin.                                         
                                    From the mutual relationships of the three sulphide phases, it
                                    appears that pyrite could have formed as a distinct phase while
                                    the sulphide in the veinlets formed at a later stage and the
                                    paragenetic sequence comprises as pyrite, chalcopyrite and then
                                    sphalerite. Late-stage deformation in the host rock has resulted in
                                    remobilization of the veinlets along minor later fractures.
                 Justification for further Considering the surface indication of mineralization in host rock
                 exploration        garnetiferous-qtz-biot-mus-schist and in associated foliated
                                    rhyolites/garnetiferous rhyolites, significant soil geochemical and
                                    geophysical anomaly on it and positive intersection of
                                    mineralized zones in two scout boreholes drilled in anomalous
                                    zones, further detailed exploration is recommended to know the
                                    total basemetal potentiality of the block area. Hence block area
                                    can be considered for CL auction..
